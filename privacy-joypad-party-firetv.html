Privacy Policy — Joypad Party for Fire TV

For the app Joypad Party (on the Amazon Appstore as “Party Games - Joypad Party”) on Amazon Fire TV.

Last updated: 22 November 2026

This Privacy Policy applies to Joypad Party for Amazon Fire TV (the “App”), published by Özcan Sarıdoğan under the Crystal Nation name. It explains what the App stores and what it sends.

Usage statistics

The App sends anonymous usage statistics to PostHog, our analytics provider (servers in the European Union), so we can see how many people play and which games they play. The statistics cover:

  • the App being opened and showing its join screen (with a running count of the screen’s game sessions, so the events of one session can be grouped);
  • a phone or the Fire TV remote joining, with the number of players and how the phone joined (its web browser or the Joypad Party phone app);
  • a game starting, a round reaching its results, or a game ending: which game, the number of players, how long it lasted, how many rounds were finished and how it ended (for example back to the games list, or everyone left);
  • which game was chosen in the games list;
  • a game asking for more phones before it can start: which game, how many phones it needed and how many were connected;
  • after a game, the optional “How was …?” question being shown, and each phone’s answer to it: the game, the answer (Loved it, OK, Not good or skipped), the chips picked (such as “Laggy” or “Too hard”) and the number of players in the room.

They include the platform (Fire TV), the App version, the language and a random ID that the App creates. PostHog receives your Fire TV’s IP address when the statistics are uploaded, as with any internet service. We use the statistics to understand and improve the App.

Switching it off: sharing anonymous usage data is on by default and can be switched off on the Fire TV with the “Usage data” tile in the games list’s About row.

Data stored on your devices

On the Fire TV, the App saves its settings, the random ID, your recently played games and your usage-data choice. Uninstalling the App deletes them.

On your phone, the controller page saves the name and colour you choose, your vibration setting and a random ID (so the Fire TV recognises your phone when it reconnects) in your web browser’s storage for the Fire TV’s address. Clearing your browser’s website data deletes them.

Your local network

The App shows a QR code and an address on the TV. Scanning it opens a controller page in your phone’s web browser. The Fire TV serves that page itself over your Wi-Fi, so nothing passes through our servers and no app or account is needed. The Fire TV also announces itself on your local network, so the Joypad Party phone app can find it. Over that connection your phone sends the Fire TV your player name, colour, random ID and controls, the text you type and the pictures you draw in a game, and your answer to “How was …?”. The Fire TV shows them to the people in the room and keeps nothing after the room closes. Other people on the same Wi-Fi who open the address or the phone app can see the TV’s name and join a game.

Purchases and ads

The App has no account, no in-app purchases or subscriptions, and no advertising.

Ratings

People can rate and review the App in the Amazon Appstore. Amazon handles ratings and reviews; we see only the public ratings and reviews people choose to post.

Children

The App is a general-audience party game and is not directed to children.

Your choices

You can switch off the usage statistics at any time as described above, and delete the App’s saved data by uninstalling the App and clearing your phone browser’s website data. For any question or request, write to us at the address below.

Changes to this policy

We may update this policy as the App evolves. Material changes will be reflected on this page with a revised “Last updated” date.
